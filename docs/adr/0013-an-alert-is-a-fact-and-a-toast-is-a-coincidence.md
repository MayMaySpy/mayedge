# An alert is a fact, and a toast is a coincidence

A row states what happened: what spiked, by how much, and what the tape showed beside it. No row carries a name for what it means. A lone spike is a row. A toast takes two spikes on one market inside two minutes, liquidations, a board move, or a market the operator holds.

The old panel scored five factors against each market's own normal and named the mix: Short squeeze, Absorption, Crowded longs. In 3.2 days it opened 1,282 rows. No label that was re-scored continued more often than a coin flip, and "price up, OI down, so shorts are covering" was a guess the data could not carry. The desk is traded by feel, on the minute, so the read belongs to the operator and the row's job is to be fast and true.

Many rows are fine when each is truly a spike; about 250 a day on the liquid board. Toasts are not, so they wait for things to line up. Funding is a state, not an event: a strip says which markets are extreme and raises nothing. Blue and orange closes are a Scan board.
